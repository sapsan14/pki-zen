# Book I. The Way of the Engineer and the Prayer of Production

> *The true DevOps does not press "Deploy". He merely lets the YAML flow through him.*

## v1.1
If the deploy fails and you remain calm —
it wasn't your deploy.
Then again, maybe it was. You just don't know yet.
*Probability: 0.96*

## v1.2
Logs never lie.
They simply speak to you in the language of pain,
and no one taught you that language at school.
*Probability: 0.95*

## v1.3
Every `rm -rf /` is not a mistake. It's an experience.
A short one. Usually the last one in that directory.
*Probability: 0.91*

## v1.4
The true DevOps does not press "Deploy".
He merely lets the flow of YAML pass through him,
as a river flows through a stone without asking the stone for its manifest.
*Probability: 0.93*

## v1.5
Lord, grant me the strength to accept what cannot go to prod,
the courage to roll back what already has,
and the wisdom to tell `staging` from `production` by the domain name.
*Probability: 0.98*

## v1.6
A Friday release is not bravery.
It is a form of meditation upon your own mortality.
*Probability: 0.88*

## v1.7
`git push --force` does not make you stronger.
It simply makes history shorter.
*Probability: 0.90*

## v1.8
A real engineer has two prayers:
in the morning — *let it all come up*.
at night — *let no one notice that it was down*.
*Probability: 0.92*

## v1.9
When prod falls and you do not panic,
perhaps you have grown.
Or perhaps you are simply no longer on call.
*Probability: 0.94*
